*	Monitor sanctions and export controls
*	Identify prohibited versus permissible activities
*	Consider reputational and end-user exposure

Scenarios I would test for ST Engineering
Scenario A: Controlled acceleration
AI capabilities continue improving but regulation, assurance and enterprise controls broadly keep 
pace.
Opportunities
*	AI-enabled engineering and design
*	Predictive maintenance
*	Autonomous inspection
*	Training and simulation
*	Cyber defence
*	Smart mobility
*	AI assurance and verification
*	Secure domain-specific AI for defence and government
Risks
*	Skills gaps
*	dependency on a few model/cloud providers
*	escalating compute costs
*	contractual liability
*	uneven adoption across BAs
Scenario B: Agentic proliferation
Autonomous agents become common in software engineering, operations, cyber and procurement.
Risks
*	Agents taking unauthorised actions
*	identity and access-control failures
*	prompt injection and tool misuse
*	agent-to-agent contagion
*	false transactions or operational commands
*	unclear responsibility for decisions
*	increased speed of cyber exploitation
Opportunity
ST Engineering could differentiate through secure, governed autonomous systems, rather than 
merely adding AI features.
Scenario C: AI geopolitical bifurcation
US-, China-, European- and sovereign-aligned AI ecosystems diverge.
Risks
*	Restricted model, GPU and cloud access
*	different regulatory and assurance requirements
*	duplicated product architectures
*	customer demands for sovereign hosting
*	restrictions on data, model weights and advanced chips
Opportunity
Modular, model-agnostic and sovereign-deployable solutions become commercially valuable.
This is consistent with your internal   2025_Geostrategic_Risks__1736989373.pdf, which describes 
digital sovereignty and AI-related supply-chain de-risking as growing government priorities. 
[2025_Geost...1736989373 | PDF]
Scenario D: Capability shock
Frontier models unexpectedly achieve large improvements in autonomous cyber, engineering or 
scientific R&D.
Potential consequences
*	Current controls become inadequate quickly
*	conventional cyber vulnerabilities are found and exploited faster
*	product development cycles compress
*	competitors emerge from outside traditional engineering sectors
*	customers demand AI capabilities faster than certification and assurance processes can 
support
Scenario E: AI disappointment or economic correction
Capability gains slow, AI returns underperform, or infrastructure economics become unattractive.
Risks
*	stranded GPU and datacentre investments
*	write-downs
*	failure to achieve productivity or revenue targets
*	staff fatigue and loss of confidence
*	abandoned pilots and vendor consolidation
This scenario is important because most corporate plans currently focus more heavily on AI 
acceleration than on underperformance.

Quantum scenarios relevant to ST Engineering
Scenario 1: No cryptographic break by 2035
The danger is complacency. Legacy systems, products and long-term contracts remain dependent on 
cryptography that may later require expensive redesign.
Scenario 2: Gradual commercial advantage
Quantum delivers targeted value in:
*	optimisation
*	logistics
*	scheduling
*	advanced material simulation
*	anomaly detection
*	sensor processing
*	maintenance planning
Opportunity is likely to emerge through hybrid classical-quantum workflows before fully general 
quantum advantage.
Scenario 3: Harvest-now-decrypt-later becomes material
Sensitive data captured today could be decrypted when sufficiently capable quantum systems 
emerge.
This particularly matters where confidentiality must endure for many years:
*	defence communications
*	technical designs
*	identity systems
*	government information
*	customer infrastructure data
*	long-life transport and aerospace platforms
Scenario 4: Cryptographic break occurs before migration is complete
Possible consequences include:
*	compromised authentication and software signing
*	untrusted firmware updates
*	exposed secure communications
*	urgent redesign of products already deployed
*	contractual and warranty disputes
*	customer loss of confidence
*	inability to support legacy systems securely
Opportunity scenario
ST Engineering could develop or integrate:
*	crypto-agile products
*	post-quantum secure communications
*	quantum-safe lifecycle services
*	quantum sensing and navigation
*	secure migration assessment
*	hybrid optimisation solutions
*	assurance services for long-life critical systems
Your internal   WEF_Global_Risks_Report_2026.pdf also highlights accelerating convergence 
between AI and quantum, rising concern over the next decade, and the need to treat quantum risk 
alongside existing cyber risks. [WEF_Global...eport_2026 | PDF], [WEF_Global...eport_2026 | PDF]
